The referee also correctly points out that there are other mechanisms for how ferroelectricity could arise in vdW materials.
In sliding ferroelectrics, such as parallel-stacked bilayers of hexagonal boron nitride or rhombohedral transition-metal dichalcogenides, the monolayers themselves lack inversion symmetry.
An inter-layer charge transfer can this occur resulting in an out-of-plane electric polarization. 
As layers slide relative to each other, the polarization gets modified, and the associated sliding modes are referred to as ferroelectric sliding modes. 

This mechanism, however, is absent in \ce{CuBr2}.
Each \ce{CuBr2} layer is itself centrosymmetric, with an inversion center at every Cu site.
Any two layers related by a translation, including an arbitrary relative sliding, therefore remain related by inversion through the midpoint between them, so interlayer sliding cannot induce a polarization. 
Both the mechanism of ferroelectricity and the nature of the collective modes in \ce{CuBr2} are therefore distinct from those of sliding ferroelectrics.
This is supported by our data: both modes soften as the temperature approaches the magnetic transition at $T_N$ and vanish above it, as expected for collective modes of the magnetic order rather than for lattice vibrations.

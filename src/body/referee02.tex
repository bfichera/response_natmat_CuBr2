The work presented by Fichera et al picks up two contemporary aspects in research on quantum materials. First, after the magnetoelectric correlations of multiferroics have been investigated for quite some time now, dynamic magnetoelectric correlations move into focus. Second, the magnetic and electric properties may become tightly entangled through fluctuations and criticality in the quantum world with a potential for novel quantum effects, especially in the low-THz frequency regime.

On this background, the authors of the present work report the observation of magnetoelectrically active Higgs modes in a layered multiferroic. Normally, these would decay immediately, but in the special combination of quantum criticality, mode energies, and spin-charge interaction they may become long-lived and thus observable.

While the observation and discussion of the author’s claims in the manuscript are quite qualitative, raising the question if explanations alternative to Higgs-mode observation may be possible, the Supplementary Information presents supporting quantitative analysis. In total, I can imagine publication of this work in Nature Materials if my following concerns can be ruled out.
